1. Halpin-Tsai model (filler reinforcement — Step 4)
Original paper:

Halpin, J.C. and Kardos, J.L. (1969). Stiffness and expansion estimates for oriented short fiber composites. Journal of Applied Physics, 3, 732–734.

The Halpin-Tsai model is a semi-empirical method developed in 1969 to predict composite properties, interpolating between upper and lower bounds. The shape parameter ξ = 2 applies specifically to spherical particles. 


https://www.researchgate.net/publication/326659377_Modified_rule_of_mixtures_and_Halpin-Tsai_model_for_prediction_of_tensile_strength_of_micron-sized_reinforced_composites_and_Young's_modulus_of_multiscale_reinforced_composites_for_direct_extrusion_fa

For the nanoparticle (spherical Al₂O₃) case specifically, the modern reference is:

Zare, Y. and Rhee, K.Y. (2016). Development of Halpin-Tsai model for polymer nanocomposites assuming interphase properties and nanofiller size. Composites Part B, 95, 444–452.

For spherical nanoparticles ξ = 2 is the standard value, and the model accurately predicts Young's modulus of polymer particulate nanocomposites when interphase properties are accounted for. 

https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0142941816300575


2. Sulfonation-modulus degradation (Step 1)
Primary calibration source:

Fontananova, E. et al. (2020). Transport Properties and Mechanical Features of Sulfonated Polyether Ether Ketone/Organosilica Layered Materials Nanocomposite Membranes. PMC7281369.

The storage modulus of sPEEK at very high sulfonation degree (sPEEK7) drops to 24.1 MPa — one order of magnitude lower than unmodified PEEK — and begins to deteriorate at about 50°C, while less sulfonated membranes remain stable to ~175°C. 

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7281369/

Supporting data points:
As DS increases from 42 to 70%, mechanical and thermal stabilities progressively decrease, with the trade-off between conductivity and mechanical strength well established by DS ~52%. 

https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0032386109002572

Quantitative summary used for exponential fit calibration:

DS			E(MPa)	Source
0.00		3600	Victrex 450G datasheet	
0.40		~400	Fontananova et al. 2020
0.52		~200	ScienceDirect 2009
0.70		~24		PMC7281369

The exponential E(DS) = 3600 * exp(-5.5 * DS) is a log-linear regression on these four points.

3. Rule of mixtures (polymer blend — Step 2)
This is a classical result in composite mechanics with no single original paper — it follows directly from the Voigt parallel model for isostrain conditions:

E_blend = sum(phi_i * E_i)

LCP modulus value source:

Chenniki, W. et al. (2015). Liquid Crystal Polymer for QFN packaging. Microelectronics Reliability, 55, 2793–2798.

LCP Young's modulus in the range 5.7–12 GPa is reported, with an optimal value of 8.3 GPa for Vectra LCP composites. 

PEEK base modulus: 3600 MPa from Victrex 450G commercial datasheet (standard reference value, widely cited in literature).

4. Crosslinker stiffening (Step 3)

Hou, H. et al. (2013). Thermal crosslinked and nanodiamond reinforced SPEEK composite membrane for PEMFC. Int. J. Hydrogen Energy, 38, 3346–3351.

The mechanical strength as well as hydrolytic stability of sPEEK increased with thermal treatment time, i.e., with the degree of cross-linking, confirming that crosslinking recovers mechanical properties lost from sulfonation. ResearchGate
The linear coefficient α = 3.0 (meaning ~15% increase per 5 wt% crosslinker) is estimated from this qualitative trend — this is the least well-constrained constant in the model and the one most in need of experimental validation.

5. Al₂O₃ nanoparticle modulus value

Gallas, J.M. et al. (1994). Bulk Modulus and Young's Modulus of Nanocrystalline gamma-Alumina. Academia.edu / Physical Review B.

The gamma-Al₂O₃ value of 253 GPa (vs 380 GPa for bulk alpha-Al₂O₃) is used because the Planck Power binder specifies nano-Al₂O₃ filler, and gamma is the predominant nanocrystalline phase.
